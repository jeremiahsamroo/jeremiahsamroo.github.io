Projects

Did congestion pricing clear the tunnels?In progress

Causal inference · synthetic control · 2026

Estimating the effect of Manhattan’s 2025 congestion pricing on tolled tunnel traffic. Built a daily panel from Port Authority crossing data and applied ridge-augmented synthetic control, with in-space and in-time placebo checks for inference. An exercise in honest measurement: the result depends on which donors you trust, so I’m diagnosing that instead of declaring a headline.

Supply-side regulatory constraints and housing affordability

Senior thesis, Princeton Economics · 2025–2026

Decomposed the Wharton Land Use Regulatory Index into mechanism-specific sub-indices (density restrictions, supply restrictions, approval delays) and estimated their effects on housing affordability across U.S. metros using 15M+ ACS/IPUMS microdata observations — cross-sectional OLS with state fixed effects. Approval delays emerged as the binding margin; the index was treated throughout as a noisy proxy for true regulatory stringency, not an instrument.

Degree saturation and housing demand

Junior paper, Princeton Economics · 2025

Tested whether excess saturation of degree holders contributes to demand-pull increases in housing CPI. Built the analysis on CPS microdata in a difference-in-differences design with high-dimensional fixed effects and clustering variants.
