Redfin home price prediction model

Independent project · R · 2025

How well can sale prices be predicted for houses in King County, Washington? This project builds a price-prediction model on ~21,600 King County home sales from May 2014 to May 2015, comparing ordinary least squares against lasso regression on log price, and testing the models on both an in-time random holdout and an out-of-time holdout of May 2015 sales.

The feature work is where most of the modeling lives. Instead of 70 separate zip-code dummies, zip codes are grouped into five price clusters with Fisher–Jenks natural breaks on median sale price, which keeps the geographic signal while cutting roughly 65 columns from the design matrix. A second feature captures a nonlinearity the data shows clearly: average log price falls once a house has 8 or more bedrooms, so the model includes a bedrooms-by-eight-plus interaction. The lasso is tuned by 10-fold cross-validation, with the champion penalty chosen by the one-standard-error rule.

The honest headline is that the lasso does not beat OLS here. At the one-standard-error penalty its test error runs slightly above OLS on both holdouts — with ~17,800 training rows against ~95 predictors, OLS barely overfits, so shrinkage buys almost no variance reduction while adding bias. That is the point of the exercise as much as the predictions themselves: regularization helps when the predictor count is large relative to the sample, and this is a case where it mostly is not.
