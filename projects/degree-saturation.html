Degree saturation and housing demand

Junior paper, Princeton Economics · 2025

Most accounts of housing costs are supply stories. This paper tests a demand-side hypothesis: that local saturation with degree holders — a concentration of high-earning, housing-hungry households — can itself be a significant contributor to demand-pull increases in housing CPI.

The design is difference-in-differences on CPS microdata, with high-dimensional fixed effects and clustering variants to isolate the effect of degree saturation on housing affordability from the places and years that simply trend together. The exercise was as much about identification discipline as the point estimate: defining the treatment so it isn’t just a proxy for income, and checking which comparison groups the estimate actually leans on.
