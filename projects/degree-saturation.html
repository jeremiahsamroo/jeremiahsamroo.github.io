Degree saturation and housing demand

Junior paper, Princeton Economics · 2025

Conventional economic investigations of proximate contributions to housing costs focus on supply-side contributors. My paper tests a demand-side hypothesis: that local saturation of college-educated degree holders, as high-earning households with excess purchasing power, can themselves be a significant contributor to demand-pull increases in housing prices in certain municipalities.

We utilized a difference-in-differences study design on CPS microdata, with a high-dimensional fixed effects estimator and clustering variants to isolate the effect of degree saturation on housing affordability, trimming away regional fixed effects and event (time) fixed effects.
