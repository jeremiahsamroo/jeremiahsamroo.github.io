Did congestion pricing clear the tunnels?In progress

Causal inference · synthetic control · 2026

In January 2025, New York switched on congestion pricing for Manhattan below 60th Street (The Congestion Relief Zone, or CRZ) in an effort to reduce peak-hour traffic. Was congestion pricing actually effective at regulating traffic? It's a hard question to answer causally, given our data constraints. We only have publicly available data for a handful of tunnels, there is no publicly available data for the untreated Manhattan, and traffic was still normalizing post-pandemic.

I built a daily panel of crossings from Metropolitan Transit Authority data, and combined it with a monthly panel of crossings from Port Authority Data to increase statistical power. I then estimated the causal effect of the congestion pricing on tolled vehicle entries into the CRZ with ridge-augmented synthetic control, validating the result with in-space placebos (permuting treatment assignment across each crossing) and an in-time placebo, by altering the timeline of treatment assignment. The project is currently in the diagnostic stages as the daily and monthly panels do not perfectly agree and the synthetic-control weights are concentrated enough that the answer is sensitive to which donors are trusted more. We need to identify which donors drive the main fit, and whether the placebo distribution truly separates the treated unit from noise. I am continually looking for additional data sources to increase the donor pool and generate more statistical power.
